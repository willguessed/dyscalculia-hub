## What is dyscalculia?

Dyscalculia is a specific learning difficulty affecting the ability to acquire arithmetical skills. Learners with dyscalculia may have difficulty understanding simple number concepts, lack an intuitive grasp of numbers, and have problems learning number facts and procedures.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Difficulty with number sense and magnitude
- Problems with counting strategies and number bonds
- Challenges with mental arithmetic and estimation
- Difficulty remembering mathematical procedures
- Struggles with time, money, and measurement concepts

## How common is dyscalculia?

Research suggests dyscalculia affects approximately **3-6% of the population**, similar to dyslexia. This means in a typical class of 30 students, 1-2 may have dyscalculia.

## Is dyscalculia the same as being "bad at maths"?

No. Dyscalculia is a **specific neurological condition** affecting numerical processing, not a result of poor teaching, lack of effort, or general low ability. Learners with dyscalculia:

- Often have average or above-average intelligence
- May excel in other areas (reading, creative subjects)
- Show persistent difficulties despite quality teaching
- Need specialized intervention approaches

## At what age can dyscalculia be identified?

Early indicators can appear in **Early Years and Key Stage 1**:

- **Age 4-5**: Difficulty counting objects, recognizing numerals
- **Age 5-6**: Struggles with number bonds, one-to-one correspondence
- **Age 7+**: Persistent problems with basic arithmetic despite intervention

Formal diagnostic assessment typically occurs from **age 6-7 onwards**, once foundational skills should be established.

## Can dyscalculia be cured?

Dyscalculia is a **lifelong condition**, but with appropriate support and intervention, individuals can develop coping strategies and improve their mathematical skills. Early identification and evidence-based intervention are crucial.

## What causes dyscalculia?

Research indicates **neurological differences** in brain regions responsible for:

- Number processing (intraparietal sulcus)
- Working memory (prefrontal cortex)
- Visual-spatial processing (parietal lobes)

**Contributing factors** may include:
- Genetic predisposition (runs in families)
- Neurodevelopmental differences
- Co-occurrence with other SpLDs (dyslexia, ADHD)

## How is dyscalculia different from maths anxiety?

| **Dyscalculia** | **Maths Anxiety** |
|---|---|
| Neurological condition | Emotional response |
| Affects number processing | Affects performance under stress |
| Present from early years | Can develop at any age |
| Requires specialized intervention | Responds to anxiety management strategies |
| Persistent across contexts | May vary by situation |

**Note**: Students with dyscalculia often *also* develop maths anxiety due to repeated failure experiences.

## Can students with dyscalculia succeed in mathematics?

**Yes!** With appropriate support:

- **Multisensory teaching** - Using concrete materials and visual representations
- **Explicit instruction** - Breaking down concepts into small steps
- **Overlearning** - Extended practice with number facts and procedures
- **Assistive technology** - Calculators, number lines, visual aids
- **Accommodations** - Extra time, reduced cognitive load

Many individuals with dyscalculia achieve mathematical competence and pursue careers requiring numeracy skills.

## What should I do if I suspect a student has dyscalculia?

Follow the **three-tier identification framework**:

1. **Universal Screening (Tier 1)** - Whole-class assessments identifying at-risk learners
2. **Targeted Assessment (Tier 2)** - More detailed testing of number sense and procedural skills
3. **Specialist Assessment (Tier 3)** - Educational psychologist diagnostic evaluation

Consult your School SENCO to initiate the assessment process.

## Are there any famous people with dyscalculia?

While fewer public figures discuss dyscalculia compared to dyslexia, notable individuals include:

- **Cher** - Singer and actress
- **Mary Tyler Moore** - Actress and activist
- **Benjamin Zephaniah** - Poet and author

Many successful individuals have dyscalculia but excel in creative, verbal, or other cognitive domains.

## Does having dyscalculia affect other areas of learning?

Dyscalculia **specifically affects numerical and arithmetical skills**. However:

- **Co-occurrence is common**: ~40% of students with dyscalculia also have dyslexia
- **Secondary impacts**: Maths anxiety, reduced confidence, avoidance behaviors
- **Cross-curricular effects**: Challenges in subjects requiring numeracy (science, geography, design technology)

Support should address both the specific learning difficulty and broader wellbeing.

## How can parents support a child with dyscalculia at home?

**Practical Strategies:**

- **Use everyday contexts** - Shopping, cooking, time-telling
- **Keep it concrete** - Physical objects, visual representations
- **Practice little and often** - Short, regular sessions rather than lengthy homework battles
- **Celebrate effort** - Focus on progress, not perfection
- **Reduce anxiety** - Make maths playful and low-stakes
- **Advocate** - Work with school to ensure appropriate support
