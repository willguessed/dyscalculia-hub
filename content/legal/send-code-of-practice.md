# SEND Code of Practice 2015 - Dyscalculia Provisions

## Overview

The SEND Code of Practice (2015) provides statutory guidance on duties, policies, and procedures relating to Part 3 of the Children and Families Act 2014. While dyscalculia is not explicitly named in the Code, mathematical difficulties and specific learning difficulties fall within the four broad areas of need.

## Relevant Statutory Framework

### Four Broad Areas of Need

The Code identifies four broad areas of special educational need:

1. **Communication and interaction**
2. **Cognition and learning** ← Dyscalculia falls here
3. **Social, emotional and mental health difficulties**
4. **Sensory and/or physical needs**

### Specific Learning Difficulties (SpLD)

From Section 6.30:

> "Support for learning difficulties may be required when children and young people learn at a slower pace than their peers, even with appropriate differentiation. Learning difficulties cover a wide range of needs, including **moderate learning difficulties (MLD)**, **severe learning difficulties (SLD)**, where children are likely to need support in all areas of the curriculum and associated difficulties with mobility and communication, through to **profound and multiple learning difficulties (PMLD)**, where children are likely to have severe and complex learning difficulties as well as a physical disability or sensory impairment.
>
> **Specific learning difficulties (SpLD)**, affect one or more specific aspects of learning. This encompasses a range of conditions such as dyslexia, dyscalculia and dyspraxia."

## Key Obligations for Schools

### Graduated Response (Assess, Plan, Do, Review)

**Section 6.44-6.56**: Schools must adopt a graduated approach to identifying and supporting SEN, known as the four-part cycle:

1. **Assess** - Clear analysis of pupil needs
2. **Plan** - Agreement of interventions and support
3. **Do** - Implementation of agreed support
4. **Review** - Evaluation of effectiveness

### High-Quality Teaching (Section 6.37)

> "Teachers are responsible and accountable for the progress and development of the pupils in their class, including where pupils access support from teaching assistants or specialist staff."

High-quality teaching, differentiated for individual pupils, is the first step in responding to pupils who have or may have SEN.

### Early Identification (Section 6.45)

> "The identification of SEN should be built into the overall approach to monitoring the progress and development of all pupils."

Schools must:
- Use data and observation to identify concerns
- Involve parents and the pupil
- Monitor progress rigorously
- Ensure concerns are not delayed

### SENCO Role (Section 6.87-6.91)

The Special Educational Needs Coordinator (SENCO) must:
- Oversee the day-to-day operation of the SEN policy
- Coordinate provision for children with SEN
- Liaise with staff, parents, and external agencies
- Advise and support teaching staff
- Maintain the SEN register

## Application to Dyscalculia

### Identification Requirements

Schools must:
1. Monitor mathematical progress for all pupils
2. Identify pupils who are not making expected progress despite quality first teaching
3. Consider whether specific mathematical difficulties indicate dyscalculia
4. Rule out alternative explanations (e.g., gaps in education, English as an additional language, general learning difficulties)

### Assessment Procedures

Schools should:
- Use screening tools to identify mathematical difficulties
- Conduct specialist assessment where dyscalculia is suspected
- Seek input from educational psychologists or specialist teachers where necessary
- Document assessment findings clearly

### Support and Intervention

Once identified, schools must:
- Provide targeted interventions matched to need
- Use evidence-based approaches (multi-sensory, concrete-to-abstract)
- Monitor progress regularly (at least termly)
- Adjust provision based on pupil response
- Involve parents in planning and review

### Recording and Monitoring

Schools must maintain:
- Clear records of concerns, assessments, and interventions
- Evidence of the graduated response (Assess, Plan, Do, Review)
- Progress data showing impact of interventions
- Consultation notes with parents and specialists

## Reasonable Adjustments (Equality Act 2010)

**Section 6.10**: Schools have duties under the Equality Act 2010 towards individual disabled pupils. Dyscalculia may constitute a disability if it has a substantial and long-term adverse effect on the ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities.

Schools must make reasonable adjustments including:
- Adjustments to teaching methods and resources
- Use of auxiliary aids (e.g., calculators, number lines)
- Additional time for tasks
- Modified assessment arrangements

## Escalation to EHC Assessment

**Section 9.14-9.16**: Where, despite relevant and purposeful action to meet SEN, a child has not made expected progress, the school or parents may request an Education, Health and Care (EHC) needs assessment.

This would be appropriate where:
- Pupil has severe and persistent difficulties
- Despite sustained intervention, progress remains significantly below age expectations
- Specialist support and resources beyond the school's capacity are needed

## Trust Responsibilities

The Ted Wragg Trust, as a multi-academy trust, must ensure:
- All schools implement the graduated response effectively
- SENCOs have appropriate training and support
- Consistent identification and assessment procedures across schools
- Evidence-based interventions are available
- Progress monitoring systems are in place
- Parental involvement is meaningful and consistent

## Compliance Monitoring

**Key Questions for Self-Review:**
- Are all staff aware of their responsibilities for pupils with dyscalculia?
- Is high-quality first teaching in mathematics consistently strong?
- Do we identify mathematical difficulties early?
- Do we use appropriate assessment tools and procedures?
- Are interventions evidence-based and well-implemented?
- Do we monitor progress rigorously and adjust provision accordingly?
- Are parents involved in planning and review?
- Do we document our graduated response clearly?
