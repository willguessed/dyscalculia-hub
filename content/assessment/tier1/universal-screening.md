# Tier 1: Universal Identification and Screening

## Purpose

Tier 1 represents **universal identification** - the systematic monitoring of all pupils' mathematical progress to identify those who may be experiencing difficulties with number and calculation. This is the foundation of our graduated response to dyscalculia.

## Key Principles

1. **All pupils are monitored** - Not just those already on the SEN register
2. **Early identification is crucial** - The earlier we identify, the better outcomes
3. **Quality First Teaching is the baseline** - Difficulties persist despite good teaching
4. **Multiple indicators** - No single test; use triangulated evidence

## Who is Responsible?

- **Class Teachers** - Day-to-day observation and monitoring
- **Mathematics Lead** - Curriculum oversight and staff support
- **SENCO** - Coordination and decision-making for escalation
- **Teaching Assistants** - Observations during support sessions

## What to Monitor

### Progress Data
- Termly mathematics assessments
- Comparison with age-related expectations
- Progress relative to starting points
- Gaps between mathematics and other subjects

### Classroom Observations
- Engagement with mathematical activities
- Response to teaching and interventions
- Reliance on concrete aids (fingers, cubes) beyond age expectations
- Speed of number processing
- Confidence and emotional responses

### Specific Indicators by Age

#### Early Years (3-5)
- Difficulty learning to count to 10
- Cannot recognise small quantities (1-3) without counting
- Struggles with "more" and "less"
- Avoids number activities

#### Key Stage 1 (5-7)
- Slow to learn number bonds to 10
- Cannot count on or back reliably
- Persistent finger counting for small numbers
- Difficulty with place value (tens and units)
- Cannot estimate quantities

#### Key Stage 2 (7-11)
- Still using fingers for basic calculations
- Cannot recall times tables despite practice
- Difficulty understanding fractions
- Struggles with multi-step problems
- Poor number sense (e.g., thinks 4 + 9 = 49)

#### Key Stage 3 (11-14)
- Persistent difficulty with basic arithmetic
- Cannot work with negative numbers
- Struggles with ratio and proportion
- Avoids mathematics classes
- Mathematics anxiety

## Screening Tools and Methods

### 1. Curriculum-Based Assessment

**What:** Regular assessment of curriculum objectives
**When:** Termly
**Who:** Class teacher

Use:
- End-of-unit assessments
- National Curriculum attainment tracking
- Progress compared to peers

**Red Flags:**
- Significantly below age-related expectations (>2 years behind)
- Little progress despite teaching
- Gap widening over time

### 2. Standardised Mathematics Tests

**What:** Norm-referenced mathematics tests
**When:** Annually
**Who:** Class teacher or SENCO

Examples:
- NFER Mathematics Tests
- Progress Test in Maths (GL Assessment)
- InCAS (Interactive Computerised Assessment System)

**Red Flags:**
- Standardised score below 85 (<16th percentile)
- Discrepancy between mathematics and reading scores
- Calculation significantly weaker than reasoning

### 3. Dyscalculia Screening Checklists

**What:** Observational checklist of dyscalculia indicators
**When:** When concerns arise
**Who:** Class teacher with SENCO

Example checklist items:
- [ ] Difficulty counting backwards
- [ ] Poor understanding of number symbols
- [ ] Cannot compare magnitudes (which is bigger: 7 or 9?)
- [ ] Slow processing of number information
- [ ] Relies on fingers for basic calculations beyond age 7
- [ ] Cannot estimate quantities
- [ ] Difficulty with number patterns
- [ ] Problems remembering mathematical facts
- [ ] Anxiety about mathematics

**Red Flags:**
- Multiple indicators present
- Persistent over time (not just a temporary struggle)
- Specific to mathematics (not general learning difficulties)

### 4. Number Sense Assessment

**What:** Quick assessments of core number skills
**When:** When screening checklist raises concerns
**Who:** SENCO or Mathematics Lead

Assess:
- **Subitising:** Show dot cards (1-6 dots) for 1 second - can they say how many without counting?
- **Number comparison:** Which is bigger: 8 or 6? 15 or 19?
- **Number line estimation:** Where would 7 go on this line from 0-10?
- **Basic facts:** Quick recall of number bonds and times tables
- **Magnitude estimation:** About how many dots? (show array of ~20)

**Red Flags:**
- Cannot subitise beyond 3
- Slow or inaccurate number comparison
- Very poor number line placement (e.g., puts 8 near 2)
- Cannot recall any basic facts fluently

## Action Points When Concerns Identified

### Step 1: Gather Evidence (2-4 weeks)
- Complete screening checklist
- Conduct number sense assessment
- Review progress data
- Observe in mathematics lessons

### Step 2: Involve Parents
- Share concerns with parents/carers
- Ask about home observations
- Check for family history of mathematical difficulties
- Explain next steps

### Step 3: Implement Targeted Support (6-8 weeks)
- Small group or 1:1 intervention
- Focus on number sense and basic facts
- Use multi-sensory, concrete methods
- Monitor progress weekly

### Step 4: Review Progress
- Has pupil made accelerated progress?
- Are gaps closing?
- Is support effective?

### Step 5: Decision Point
- **If good progress:** Continue support, monitor, and gradually fade
- **If limited progress:** Escalate to Tier 2 specialist assessment

## Escalation Criteria to Tier 2

Refer to Tier 2 if:
- Pupil is >2 years behind in mathematics despite Quality First Teaching
- Targeted intervention (6-8 weeks) has minimal impact
- Significant discrepancy between mathematics and other subjects
- Multiple dyscalculia indicators present
- Parental concern about mathematical difficulties
- Pupil experiencing significant anxiety or distress

## Timescales

- **Initial concern raised** → Evidence gathering begins within 1 week
- **Evidence gathering** → 2-4 weeks
- **Parental involvement** → Within 2 weeks of concern
- **Targeted support** → Begins within 2 weeks of decision
- **Review of support** → After 6-8 weeks
- **Tier 2 referral** → If limited progress, within 1 term of initial concern

## Recording and Documentation

Maintain clear records of:
- Date concern first noted
- Evidence gathered (assessments, observations)
- Parental consultation notes
- Interventions implemented
- Progress monitoring data
- Decision and rationale for next steps

Use: **Tier 1 Intervention Record Sheet** (see Resources)

## Success Indicators

Effective Tier 1 identification means:
- All pupils' mathematical progress is monitored systematically
- Concerns are identified early (not left until KS3 or later)
- Evidence-based decisions about escalation
- Parents involved from the start
- Clear documentation of graduated response

---

**Remember:** Tier 1 is not about diagnosing dyscalculia - it's about identifying pupils who need additional support and gathering evidence to inform next steps.
